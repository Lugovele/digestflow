# LinkedIn Post Quality Target

## Purpose

This document defines the editorial quality target for generated LinkedIn posts in PostFlow.

The goal is not generic professional content.

The goal is a post with:

- one clear point of view;
- strong opening;
- concrete reader problem;
- human voice;
- early pattern interrupt;
- specific evidence;
- practical value;
- one clear CTA.

## Reference Pattern

PostFlow should use these as editorial heuristics, not guaranteed algorithm facts:

- hook in the first line, ideally first 8-12 words;
- pattern interrupt in the first third;
- concrete details instead of abstractions;
- honest observation, doubt, or revision of thinking;
- topical consistency;
- no external links in the body;
- one open CTA.

## Required Structure

Recommended spine:

```text
hook
-> context
-> pattern interrupt
-> core tension
-> evidence or example
-> author insight
-> practical takeaway
-> open CTA
```

This is a spine, not a rigid template.

## Hook

Good hooks create immediate relevance, tension, or curiosity. They should make the reader understand why the post matters before the post starts explaining.

Good hook types:

- personal action with concrete detail;
- recognizable reader pain with unexpected cause;
- counterintuitive claim;
- sharp observation;
- specific result or contrast.

Bad hooks:

- "AI is changing everything"
- "In today's fast-paced world"
- "Many people are talking about..."
- "Here are some thoughts about..."
- "The future of work is here"

## Pattern Interrupt

The post should shift early from expected framing to sharper argument.

Examples:

- "But the real problem is not the tool."
- "The interesting part is what happened before that."
- "I used to think this was a prompt problem. It wasn't."
- "The failure usually starts before anyone opens the AI tool."

## Concrete Reader Problem

Weak:

```text
Teams struggle with AI adoption.
```

Better:

```text
Teams add AI tools on top of unclear handoffs, missing decision rules, and undefined ownership.
```

Weak:

```text
Automation can be difficult.
```

Better:

```text
The workflow breaks when nobody knows what input the AI should trust or when a human should step in.
```

## One Controlling Angle

The controlling angle is the main claim the post is built around.

Examples:

```text
AI automation fails when the workflow underneath is still vague.
```

```text
The bottleneck does not disappear when teams add AI. It moves to the workflow design layer.
```

```text
A good AI system starts before the prompt, with the process it is supposed to operate inside.
```

Bad angle behavior:

- several unrelated points;
- broad summary of articles;
- generic AI adoption commentary;
- source terminology becoming the main angle by accident;
- list of facts without a position.

## Evidence

Evidence supports the angle.

Evidence can be:

- fact;
- number;
- example;
- source pattern;
- contrast;
- workflow observation.

Evidence should not dominate the post. The post should not sound like a research summary.

Bad:

```text
Article A says X. Article B says Y. Article C says Z.
```

Good:

```text
The pattern across the sources is not tool adoption. It is integration failure.
```

## Author Point Of View

The author interprets facts instead of just reporting them.

When the current PostFlow authorial directive requires explicit personal
presence, maximum Author Point Of View requires exactly one author-owned
interpretive statement: a statement that visibly assigns an evidence-derived
judgment to the author without inventing biography, experience, credentials,
clients, direct exposure, or emotion.

Author-owned interpretation is separate from Human Voice. A post can sound
natural while still lacking explicit ownership of the judgment.

Useful examples:

```text
I don't think this is mainly a model capability problem.
```

```text
The workflow has to be legible before it can be automated.
```

```text
A faster broken process is still a broken process.
```

Avoid:

- neutral summary;
- consultant-speak;
- generic motivational tone;
- exaggerated claims;
- fake personal stories.

## Human Voice

The post should sound like a real person with a clear thought, not like a corporate summary, consultant memo, generic AI text, or article recap.

Human voice can come from:

- natural human phrasing;
- clear author interpretation;
- honest observation, doubt, tension, or revision of thinking;
- specific wording instead of polished abstractions;
- readable LinkedIn voice;
- text that sounds written by a person, not by a content template.

Avoid:

- corporate or institutional tone;
- consultant-speak;
- generic AI-generated phrasing;
- motivational filler;
- overly polished but empty language;
- article-summary tone;
- vague abstractions;
- safe wording that avoids a real position.

## Practical Value

Each post should give one practical takeaway.

Good:

```text
Before automating, define the trigger, input, decision, output, owner, and exception.
```

```text
Do not start with the AI tool. Start with the handoff that currently breaks.
```

```text
If a human cannot explain the decision rule, AI will not magically make it reliable.
```

Bad:

```text
Use AI strategically.
```

```text
Think about your workflows.
```

```text
Stay ahead of the curve.
```

## CTA

The post should have one CTA, usually an open question.

Good:

```text
Where does your workflow still break before AI even enters the picture?
```

```text
What would you clarify before trying to automate this?
```

```text
Which part of the process would you not trust AI with yet?
```

Bad:

```text
Follow me for more.
```

```text
Like and share.
```

```text
Book a call.
```

## Length And Formatting

Rules:

- under 1300 characters because the current validator enforces this limit;
- short paragraphs;
- no dense blocks;
- readable on LinkedIn;
- no external links in the body.

## Quality Rubric

Score each post from 1 to 5 on each criterion.

| Criterion            | Max score |
| -------------------- | --------: |
| Hook                 |         5 |
| Controlling Angle    |         5 |
| Reader Problem       |         5 |
| Pattern Interrupt    |         5 |
| Evidence             |         5 |
| Author Point Of View |         5 |
| Human Voice          |         5 |
| Practical Value      |         5 |
| CTA                  |         5 |
| Total                |        45 |

### Hook

- 5 = first line is specific, sharp, and creates immediate curiosity or tension; ideally works within the first 8-12 words.
- 4 = clear and relevant hook, but slightly less sharp or less specific.
- 3 = understandable opening, but ordinary or predictable.
- 2 = slow, broad, or too explanatory.
- 1 = generic opening such as "AI is changing everything" or no real hook.

### Controlling Angle

- 5 = one strong controlling angle, visible throughout the post.
- 4 = clear angle, with minor dilution.
- 3 = angle exists but competes with secondary points.
- 2 = unclear angle or angle shifts during the post.
- 1 = no clear angle; reads like summary or generic commentary.

### Reader Problem

- 5 = concrete, recognizable, and tied to a real workflow/business situation.
- 4 = clear and relevant but could be more specific.
- 3 = understandable but broad.
- 2 = vague pain point with little operational detail.
- 1 = missing or generic.

### Pattern Interrupt

- 5 = early meaningful shift that reframes the expected topic.
- 4 = clear shift, but not very surprising.
- 3 = mild contrast or turn.
- 2 = weak or late shift.
- 1 = no pattern interrupt.

### Evidence

- 5 = specific evidence clearly supports the angle without taking over the post.
- 4 = relevant evidence supports the angle, but could be more concrete.
- 3 = evidence exists but is generic or loosely connected.
- 2 = weak evidence; mostly assertion.
- 1 = unsupported claims, invented facts, or article-summary structure.

### Author Point Of View

- 5 = exactly one explicit author-owned interpretive statement; substantive choice between competing readings; judgment tied to supplied evidence; no fabricated personal context.
- 4 = visible point of view or strong article-like authored judgment, but limited or absent explicit personal presence.
- 3 = strong thesis without clear ownership, summary with rhetorical framing, or generic first-person marker without substantive judgment.
- 2 = mostly neutral reporting.
- 1 = no author position.

### Human Voice

- 5 = sounds natural, specific, and human; avoids corporate or template phrasing.
- 4 = mostly human and readable, with minor generic phrasing.
- 3 = understandable but somewhat generic or polished.
- 2 = corporate, summary-like, or overly safe.
- 1 = clearly template-like, generic AI text, consultant-speak, or no human voice.

Personal presence must not automatically increase Human Voice. Human Voice
scores naturalness, rhythm, clarity, non-corporate language, and non-generic
prose.

### Practical Value

- 5 = specific actionable takeaway the reader can apply.
- 4 = useful takeaway but slightly broad.
- 3 = generally useful but not very concrete.
- 2 = generic advice.
- 1 = no practical value.

### CTA

- 5 = one open question that naturally follows from the post and invites thoughtful response.
- 4 = one relevant question, but ordinary.
- 3 = acceptable CTA but generic.
- 2 = weak, pushy, or slightly disconnected CTA.
- 1 = missing CTA, multiple CTAs, or engagement bait.

## Pass Criteria

A post passes if:

- total score is at least 36 out of 45;
- hook score is at least 4;
- controlling angle score is at least 4;
- evidence score is at least 3;
- author point of view score is at least 4;
- human voice score is at least 4;
- there are no unsupported claims;
- there is only one CTA;
- there are no links in the body;
- post does not exceed 1300 characters.

## Automatic Fail Conditions

A post fails automatically if it:

- invents facts;
- invents cases;
- invents metrics;
- invents personal experience;
- uses external links in the body;
- has no clear angle;
- reads like a summary of articles;
- uses unsupported causal strengthening;
- sounds like generic AI-generated content;
- reads like a corporate memo instead of a human LinkedIn post;
- has no human author voice;
- makes source terminology the main angle by accident;
- exceeds 1300 characters;
- relies on generic phrases as the main argument;
- has no required CTA in post_text;
- has more than one CTA;
- leaks internal process language into reader-facing text.
